Appendix A. External Benchmark: DLawBench (Adapted Run)

This appendix continues the report after Section 8 and the References. It reports the first external run of the LexFlow consultation policies on DLawBench, a public bilingual benchmark for multi-turn legal consultation. The run is an adaptation, not an official leaderboard entry: the benchmark's rubrics, metric formulas and denominators are used unchanged, with deterministic offline client and judge implementations.

A.1 From two scripted claim types to a public protocol

Section 7 listed the public-benchmark comparison as a qualitative limitation. DLawBench provides 461 Chinese and U.S. cases, four client personas, paired client-belief and court-record facts, and expert inquiry and memo rubrics [2]. We use the Chinese split and the P_WD persona to ask a narrow transfer question: can the same gate → ask → memo control flow operate on criminal, administrative, bankruptcy, company, labour, unfair-competition and tort consultations without changing the benchmark's scoring? The result is a pipeline and behavioural result, not a claim of general legal capability.

A.2 Setup and scoring

  • Benchmark. DLawBench public release, commit 9f6325f; cases_cn.jsonl; 30 Chinese cases shipped in the release's CN split (the paper's full corpus is larger); at most 10 dialogue turns.
  • Policies. answer_first writes a memo from the opening message and asks no questions; gate_only runs a six-question domain intake and then writes the memo; full asks up to nine questions and adds per-fact provenance and a verification section.
  • Sessions. 3 policies × 30 cases = 90 with P_WD; a second 90 with P_CD gives 180 sessions in total.
  • Lawyer shim. An OpenAI-compatible server exposes the policy to DLawBench's talk_to_client / write_memo protocol. It sees only the opening and the client's answers; it never opens the case file, hidden facts, or evaluation_criteria.
  • Scoring. Sessions are scored through the official dlawbench.evaluate.evaluate_session and the official scripts/metrics.py formulas. To keep the run reproducible offline, the judge is a deterministic lexical rubric matcher that answers the same official prompts; the rubric, formulas and denominators remain those of the benchmark.
  • Aggregation. Each cell is a mean with a 95% percentile interval from 5,000 case-cluster bootstrap resamples (seed 20261124). Cases, not sessions, are resampled, so the three policies share the same resampled case set.

A.3 The seven metrics

The benchmark reports seven consultation-quality metrics. Fact Coverage is discovery_rate (covered facts / all facts); Inquiry is EAS (mean expected-action rubric score); Elicitation is their mean. Fact Resolution is absolute_accuracy (correctly analysed facts / all facts); Issue Resolution is MES (mean memo-expectation rubric score); Resolution is their mean. Fidelity is grounding_rate (grounded claims / total factual claims). The required-section score (FFS) is a format filter and is not a ranking metric.

A.4 Results

Table A1 reports the main P_WD matrix. Gating is the dominant effect: Fact Coverage rises from 0.016 to 0.628 and Inquiry from 0.000 to 0.216; Elicitation rises from 0.008 to 0.422. The full policy adds three turns and reaches 0.696, 0.228 and 0.462 respectively. Issue Resolution moves less (0.295 → 0.378 / 0.399) because all three policies share the same domain-level issue library; the gain comes from selecting issues with elicited facts, not from new legal knowledge.

A.5 What the run establishes

The adapted run establishes three results. First, the full gate → ask → memo control flow runs end-to-end on a public multi-turn protocol with 30 unseen Chinese cases: all 180 sessions completed with a memo, and every session was scored by the benchmark's own evaluator. Second, gating moves the behavioural metrics by a large margin — Fact Coverage from 0.016 to 0.628 (gate_only) and 0.696 (full), Elicitation from 0.008 to 0.422 and 0.462, and Resolution from 0.155 to 0.496 and 0.541. The persona extension moves in the same direction: P_CD lowers Fact Coverage to 0.457 / 0.583 while Inquiry stays unchanged, showing that disclosure difficulty affects what the lawyer can learn. Third, the result is reproducible from one command, with seven metrics, case-cluster confidence intervals, and a hash-checked vendored scorer.

Scope. The evaluation uses the public CN split and a fixed 10-turn budget. The reported quantities are the policy deltas on that split, computed with the benchmark's own seven metrics and case-cluster confidence intervals.

A.6 Reproducibility

python external/dlawbench/run.py --n-cases 30 --bootstrap 5000
python external/dlawbench/run.py --n-cases 30 --personas P_CD \
    --output-dir runs/external/pcd --bootstrap 5000
python external/dlawbench/verify.py
python external/dlawbench/verify.py runs/external/pcd

The run writes summary.md, scores.csv, leaderboard.md and run_manifest.json under runs/external/ (and runs/external/pcd/). The manifest records the DLawBench commit, the LexFlow commit, the MIT / CC BY 4.0 licences, the case-file hash, n, and the exact command. Vendored DLawBench files are hash-checked against the pinned commit; verify.py also scans the shim for any reference to rubric or hidden-case fields. A one-page method note is in docs/EXTERNAL_EVAL.md.

A.7 Takeaway

Taken together, the appendix converts the report's qualitative public-benchmark comparison into a measured, reproducible pipeline result. The mechanism that travels is the gate; the next evaluation step is an official LLM judge and the full case split. The adapted run is therefore best read as an existence proof and a reusable harness for the next evaluation round.
